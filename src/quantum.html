Going Quantum

Quantum Technologies are an emerging field that uses the physics learned in the twentieth century for practical computational problems. To make headway in this space, a good team requires knowledge of atomic physics, quantum mechanics, linear algebra, vector calculus, quantum chemistry, information technology, logic design, programming and the application of algorithms. Therefore, it should not come as a surprise that it takes a team of geniuses to design and find use cases for these systems that are so powerful, we are still finding use cases for them, and I want to be a part of this story. As I went through school, I had four standout subjects: Mathematics, Physics, IT and Chemistry. How could I not appreciate the universality of Physics, the recipes of Chemistry, the ubiquity of technology or the language of Math that bound them all together? The problem arose in High School where only three could be taken at high level with the fourth relegated to standard. I had to make a choice and ultimately, Chemistry drew the short straw so that I could focus on a career in electrical engineering. Then one serendipitious day, while researching modern technologies for my IT class in 2021, I encountered two words: Quantum computing. This was the first time I had encountered the word "Quantum". It means a discrete packet. However, the impact it had in my life was very analog. Here was the perfect fusion of my four favorite subjects in a field so nascent that I had the chance to get on the train just as it left the station. My response was immediate. I found a book: Quantum computing Progress and Prospects from the National Academies Press and went to work understanding the technology. In hindsight, the book was way over my head at the time but the more I learned, the more I wanted to learn. I enrolled in Qubit by Qubit summer camp and wrote a poor research essay attempting to justify why Quantum technology will define the next generation of computers. Once the essay was finished in late 2021, I entered my quantum winter. Some gifted people are able to juggle their passions and schoolwork, or have their passions supported by their school work, but my priorities shifted when I enrolled in Union University. I was an electrical engineer in training and that should have been it. However, in the Fall of 2023, I picked up a minor in Physics. I had liked the classes well enough and having lost Chemistry, I was not willing to lose it too. This seemingly small decision had a massive impact on the rest of my college journey because it led me to Quantum Mechanics, Spring 2023. With the beautiful elegance of the Hydrogen atom revealed, the quantum winter was over. Now that I had an undergraduate-level understanding of the physical qubit layer, the race was on to find a way to break into the industry. Studying electromagnetics that Fall gave me that in by going into detail about photon travel. This is crucial to know to understand quantum gates and flying qubits.

My first encounter with engineering was in 2010 when I teamed up with a friend to participate in an invention convention.

We came up with the idea of a magnetic bookshelf, one capable of reading magnetic strips on books moving them to a target position.

Using household objects, we took first place thanks to a proof of concept.

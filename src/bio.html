Shane was born in Spring 2003 in the United Kingdom.
In 2009, his family moved to Uganda where he would spent a lot of time transitioning between schools. His first encounter with engineering was in 2010 when he teamed up with a friend to participate in an invention convention. The two came up with the idea of a magnetic bookshelf, one capable of reading magnetic strips on books and using those to move the book to its target position. This idea was born from Shane's love of books, something he keeps today. While the complexity of such an idea was far beyond the ability of a 7-year old, with a bookshelf from his home and fridge magnets from his friend's, the two managed to make first place, sparking Shane's interest in science and inventing.
In 2012, Shane enrolled in a different elementary school, one which followed Uganda's National curriculum and his proficiency in Math only grew. In 2014, he, alongside others represented his school in a National Mathematics competition, something he would do again in 2015. Also in 2014, he did the Mathematics final exam for the grade above him and scored 96%. In the same year, he pursued his interest in computers and had his first encounter with coding: With Scratch. At the end of the bootcamp, he understood the fundamentals of circuits and coding. In 2015, he graduated from middle school with an aggregate score of 6, equivalent to 2 A's and 2 B's in the 4 subjects studied.
In 2016, Shane enrolled in St. Mary's College Kisubi, a leading school in Uganda, where he studied a host of new subjects and his affinity for the sciences would only grow. He was introduced to Physics, Chemistry and computer science, which would be necessary in a career focused on computers, alongside subjects whose value he would not appreciate until much later: Enterpreneurship, Technical Drawing and others. While at this school, Shane also honed in on his chess skills, while attempting to join the various coding and robotics clubs, however, opportunities were few and far between but this did not stiffle his passion. He did, however, join the debate club which was much more active. He managed to compete in the World Scholars' Cup but was unable to go to the second round because he had final exams to do. He would ultimately graduate from this school in 2019, with an aggregate score of 9 in his 8 best subjects, equivalent to 7 A's and 1 B.
2020 was the year in which Shane's preparation was met with opportunity. He enrolled in the Aga Khan Academy, Mombasa to study the International Baccalaureate and right away, it was a perfect fit. He took High Level Physics, Information Technology in Global Society and Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches. At Standard Level, he took French, English and Chemistry (which he seriously considered taking at High level). He became a dorm captain at this academy and was responsible for coordinating residential activities and making sure all boys aged 12-14 had a comfortable transition. While here, he also accepted an internship with the Aga Khan Development Netork, which was responsibe for maintaining the campus area networks at locations, as well as took on a job designing the website for one Scorpius Pharmacy. He also wrote a 4000-word essay on the topic "To what extent will IBM's quantum system One impact scientific research at CERN?" and attended Qubit by Qubit summer camp in 2021. After a string of 2nd honors awards at this academy, Shane graduated with 41 out of a potential 45 points.
Since 2022, Shane has been enrolled in Union University in Jackson, Tennessee where he majors in electrical and electronics engineering while double minoring in Physics and Mathematics, all of which he deems will be necessary in building a career. He joined the student activites council and student government, so he is always bust making sure his classmates are having a comfortable time. He is relatively flexible working in most states, as long as he is doing something that interests him and that he can use to make people smile. When asked why he chose engineering, he said, "Growing up, I had 2 major things that kept me entertained: LEGO and video games. Now I want to help make better computers to spread my smile to others."
